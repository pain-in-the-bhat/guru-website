# Blog Backlog Expansion Plan

Source: `Blog Backlog- Notes from an AI-Accelerated Midwit.pdf`

## The Collection Thesis

The recurring question is not "what can AI do?"

It is:

> What happens to human ambition, taste, identity, and knowledge work when intelligence becomes abundant but wisdom stays scarce?

Most of the backlog sits inside that question. The strongest posts should avoid generic AI optimism or doom. The house voice is participant-observer: a smart friend admitting he is implicated in the behavior he is critiquing.

## Recommended Publishing Order

   - Best bridge from the existing posts.
   - Extends `Usage Anxiety`, `Harness`, and `System That Eats Itself`.
   - High relevance because it turns cost into behavior, not just economics.

2. **The Goldilocks Conundrum for AI Enthusiasts**

3. **smolgate**

   - Likely very relatable for tool-heavy AI Twitter / productivity people.

5. **The Aesthetics of Intelligence**
   - A cultural essay, less technical.
   - Good for variety after several workflow-heavy pieces.

6. **Shoutout to My X**
   - Breaks out of pure AI and brings internet culture / identity into the site.

7. **The OpenClaw Great Divide**
   - Needs either a stronger personal anecdote or more concrete stakes before drafting.

8. **The Impossible Homework of Modern Tech**
   - Good personal angle, but risks becoming broad unless anchored in one day/week of trying to catch up.

9. **Identifying AI Snake Oil Salesmen for Dummies**
   - Funny, but needs care. Avoid dunking. Make it about incentives, not villains.

10. **The Productivity Bodybuilders**

11. **Humans After Intelligence Becomes Cheap**
   - Save as a later umbrella essay after more posts exist.

## Voice Rules for Expansion

- Start from a specific lived moment whenever possible.
- Admit complicity. The narrator should be caught doing the thing he is analyzing.
- Avoid "everyone is stupid" energy.
- Prefer psychological diagnosis over moral judgment.
- Keep the jokes dry and occasional.
- Make the final turn land on human behavior, not tool commentary.
