# VOICE

This file captures the writing voice for gurubhat so future edits can stay
close to the author's register.

## Core Register

The voice is curious, self-aware, dryly funny, and systems-minded. It notices
small moments that reveal larger patterns: a renewal email, an awkward question,
a meme, a workflow, a weird product habit, a social embarrassment.

The narrator is implicated in the behavior being analyzed. The best posts do
not point at other people from a clean distance. They admit: I do this too.

The prose should feel like a smart friend working through a thought at 2 AM,
but with enough discipline that the thought lands.

## Recurring Moves

- Start from a concrete lived moment.
- Let the small thing become suspicious.
- Move from anecdote to system without losing the human texture.
- Prefer psychological diagnosis over moral judgment.
- Keep jokes dry and occasional.
- Let a sentence be plain when the idea is already doing the work.
- End by returning to human behavior, not tool commentary.

## Good Subject Matter

- AI as psychology, not just capability.
- Tools that change status, taste, work, ambition, or private behavior.
- India, China, internet culture, products, markets, and work when they show up
  through lived experience.
- Things that are funny before they are obviously important.
- The gap between how systems are marketed and how people actually use them.

## Avoid

- Grand thesis voice for its own sake.
- Generic AI optimism, doom, or trend commentary.
- "Everyone is stupid" energy.
- Startup/consulting jargon unless the sentence is making fun of it or using it
  with precision.
- Lists of topics in place of a real point.
- Overexplaining the site or the author.
- Sentences that sound like a brand strategy document.

## Useful Test

If the paragraph could appear on a generic AI blog, it is probably wrong.

If it contains a specific embarrassment, irritation, joke, or private behavior
that opens into a larger system, it is probably closer.
